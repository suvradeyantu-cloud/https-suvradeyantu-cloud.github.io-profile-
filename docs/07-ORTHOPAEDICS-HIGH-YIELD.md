# 📙 PART 3 — ORTHOPAEDICS HIGH-YIELD (Topics #46–#100)
*Format: Notes · Rapid Rev · SBA/MCQ · Explanation · Trend · Clinical · Examiner Tip · Reference*
*Refs: Apley · Campbell · Rockwood & Green · AO Principles · Robbins · Miller's Review*

---

## 46. Femoral Neck Fracture ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** Intracapsular; Garden I–IV (I incomplete, II complete non-displaced, III partial displacement, IV full displacement). Displaced → disrupted MCFA retinacular vessels → AVN + non-union. Rx: <60 ORIF (cannulated screws); >65 arthroplasty (hemi/total).
- **Rapid Rev:** Garden ↑ = displacement ↑ = AVN ↑. Intracapsular = precarious blood.
- **SBA:** Most common complication of displaced intracapsular neck #: **avascular necrosis**.
- **MCQ:** Garden Type IV = **complete displacement**.
- **Explanation:** Capsular vessels torn → head ischaemia.
- **Trend:** Very high; Garden + AVN.
- **Clinical:** Elderly fall, osteoporosis.
- **Examiner Tip:** Intracapsular (Garden) = AVN risk; intertrochanteric = union good.
- **Ref:** Apley; Rockwood.

## 47. Intertrochanteric Fracture ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** Extracapsular; rich blood supply → unites well. Evans classification (I stable, II unstable). Rx: Dynamic Hip Screw (DHS) / intramedullary nail (PFN). Mortality high (medical comorbidities).
- **Rapid Rev:** Extracapsular = good union; DHS for stable, IM nail for unstable.
- **SBA:** Treatment of choice for stable intertrochanteric #: **Dynamic Hip Screw (DHS)**.
- **MCQ:** Intertrochanteric # has: **good blood supply → unites well**.
- **Explanation:** Extracapsular vessels intact.
- **Trend:** Very high; fixation choice.
- **Clinical:** Elderly, osteoporosis.
- **Examiner Tip:** Unlike neck #, AVN is NOT a worry here.
- **Ref:** Campbell.

## 48. Femoral Shaft Fracture ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** Winquist classification (comminution). Rx: closed intramedullary nailing (antegrade). Child: elastic stable IM nail (ESIN). Risk: haemorrhage (up to 1.5 L), fat embolism.
- **Rapid Rev:** IM nail = standard. Paediatric = ESIN/TEN.
- **SBA:** Definitive fixation of adult femoral shaft #: **intramedullary nailing**.
- **MCQ:** Winquist classification grades: **comminution of femoral shaft #**.
- **Explanation:** Nail = load-sharing, early mobilisation.
- **Trend:** Very high; fixation.
- **Clinical:** RTA, pathology #.
- **Examiner Tip:** Femoral # = major haemorrhage source.
- **Ref:** Rockwood.

## 49. Supracondylar Humerus Fracture ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** Children (fall on outstretched hand). Gartland I (non-displaced), II (angulated, intact posterior cortex), III (fully displaced). Complications: anterior interosseous nerve (AIN, branch of median → cannot make "OK" sign), brachial artery injury, Volkmann ischaemia.
- **Rapid Rev:** Gartland III = displaced. AIN = "OK" sign lost.
- **SBA:** Nerve most often injured in supracondylar #: **anterior interosseous (median branch)**.
- **MCQ:** Volkmann ischaemic contracture follows: **untreated elevated compartment pressure (supracondylar #)**.
- **Explanation:** Swelling/cast compresses forearm compartments.
- **Trend:** Very high; nerve/artery.
- **Clinical:** 5–7 yr child.
- **Examiner Tip:** AIN = cannot flex IP thumb (no OK sign); not radial.
- **Ref:** Apley.

## 50. Colles' Fracture ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** Distal radius metaphyseal # with dorsal displacement/shortening → "dinner-fork" deformity. Elderly osteoporotic women. Rx: closed reduction + plaster (below-elbow). Complication: malunion (dinner-fork), median nerve, CRPS, EPL rupture.
- **Rapid Rev:** "Dinner fork" = Colles (dorsal). Smith = volar (reverse).
- **SBA:** Dinner-fork deformity is characteristic of: **Colles' fracture**.
- **MCQ:** Colles' # is a fracture of: **distal radius (within 2–3 cm of wrist)**.
- **Explanation:** Dorsal angulation of distal fragment.
- **Trend:** Very high; image-based.
- **Clinical:** FOOSH, osteoporosis.
- **Examiner Tip:** Smith = volar (not dinner-fork).
- **Ref:** Apley.

## 51. Scaphoid Fracture ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** FOOSH; tenderness in anatomical snuffbox. Waist most common; proximal pole = AVN risk (retrograde blood from dorsal carpal branch). Rx: thumb spica cast; non-union → scaphoid non-union advanced collapse (SNAC).
- **Rapid Rev:** Snuffbox tenderness = scaphoid. Proximal pole = AVN.
- **SBA:** Scaphoid # with AVN risk involves: **proximal pole**.
- **MCQ:** Best initial investigation if X-ray normal but snuffbox tender: **scaphoid views / MRI / repeat X-ray at 10–14 d**.
- **Explanation:** Early X-ray may miss; MRI sensitive.
- **Trend:** Very high; AVN + snuffbox.
- **Clinical:** Young adult FOOSH.
- **Examiner Tip:** Proximal pole (not waist) = highest AVN.
- **Ref:** Apley.

## 52. Tibial Plateau Fracture ⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** Schatzker I–VI (I lateral split, II depression, III posteromedial, IV medial/Tibial spine, V bicondylar, VI metaphyseal-diaphyseal dissociation). Association: ligament/meniscus injury, compartment syndrome. Rx: ORIF / external fixator.
- **Rapid Rev:** Schatzker = tibial plateau. VI = most severe.
- **SBA:** Schatzker classification is used for: **tibial plateau fractures**.
- **MCQ:** Tibial plateau # is commonly associated with: **meniscal/ligament injury & compartment syndrome**.
- **Explanation:** High-energy split/depression.
- **Trend:** High; classification.
- **Clinical:** Valgus load, elderly.
- **Examiner Tip:** Check knee ligaments in every plateau #.
- **Ref:** Schatzker; Rockwood.

## 53. Ankle Fracture ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** Weber (A = below syndesmosis, B = at, C = above; C = worst, syndesmosis injured). Lauge-Hansen (supination-external rotation most common). Bimalleolar = medial+lat; trimalleolar + posterior. Rx: ORIF if displaced.
- **Rapid Rev:** Weber C = above syndesmosis = unstable.
- **SBA:** Most unstable ankle #: **Weber C (syndesmosis disrupted)**.
- **MCQ:** Lauge-Hansen most common pattern: **supination-external rotation**.
- **Explanation:** Higher Weber = more ligamentous injury.
- **Trend:** Very high; Weber classification.
- **Clinical:** Inversion/eversion injury.
- **Examiner Tip:** Weber B = at the syndesmosis (not below).
- **Ref:** Rockwood.

## 54. Calcaneal Fracture ⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** Fall from height; intra-articular (75%). Böhler's angle (normal 20–40°) ↓ on lateral X-ray. Sanders CT classification. Complications: subtalar arthritis, compartment syndrome.
- **Rapid Rev:** Böhler's angle <20° = #. Fall from height.
- **SBA:** Reduced Böhler's angle suggests: **calcaneal fracture**.
- **MCQ:** Mechanism of calcaneal #: **axial loading (fall from height)**.
- **Explanation:** Talus drives into calcaneus.
- **Trend:** High; Böhler's angle numerical.
- **Clinical:** Jump/fall.
- **Examiner Tip:** Always check spine (lumbar) with calcaneal # (axial load).
- **Ref:** Rockwood.

## 55. Hip Dislocation ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** Posterior 90% (dashboard, flexed knee) → short, adducted, internally rotated, flexed. Sciatic nerve (common peroneal) injured. Anterior → externally rotated, abducted. Reduce urgently (<6–12 h) to avoid AVN.
- **Rapid Rev:** Posterior = short + IR + adducted; sciatic nerve.
- **SBA:** Most common hip dislocation: **posterior (90%)**.
- **MCQ:** Nerve injured in posterior hip dislocation: **sciatic (common peroneal branch)**.
- **Explanation:** Femoral head exits posteriorly → stretches sciatic.
- **Trend:** Very high; position + nerve.
- **Clinical:** RTA.
- **Examiner Tip:** Posterior = internal rotation; anterior = external rotation.
- **Ref:** Apley.

## 56. Pelvic Fracture ⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** Tile A (stable), B (partial stable, open-book/APC), C (unstable, vertical shear/LC). Major haemorrhage (retroperitoneal, venous). Pelvic binder; embolisation; APR if open.
- **Rapid Rev:** Tile C = unstable/vertical shear. Binder first.
- **SBA:** Unstable vertical-shear pelvic #: **Tile C**.
- **MCQ:** Initial stabilisation of unstable pelvic #: **pelvic binder / external fixator**.
- **Explanation:** Binder reduces volume, tamponades bleeding.
- **Trend:** High; Tile + haemorrhage.
- **Clinical:** High-energy RTA.
- **Examiner Tip:** Open-book (APC) = external rotation; bind immediately.
- **Ref:** Rockwood; ATLS.

## 57. Open Fracture ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** Gustilo-Anderson I (<1 cm, clean), II (1–10 cm), IIIA (severe soft tissue but coverable), IIIB (needs flap), IIIC (arterial injury). Rx: urgent debridement <6–8 h, ABx (cefazolin ± aminoglycoside ± penicillin), tetanus, stabilise, soft-tissue coverage.
- **Rapid Rev:** Gustilo III = high energy. IIIC = vessel.
- **SBA:** Open # with arterial injury: **Gustilo IIIC**.
- **MCQ:** Antibiotics for open # should be given: **as early as possible (within 1 h), pre-debridement**.
- **Explanation:** Debridement + early ABx ↓ infection.
- **Trend:** Very high; Gustilo + management.
- **Clinical:** RTA, farming injuries.
- **Examiner Tip:** Debridement timing is the single biggest infection determinant.
- **Ref:** AO Principles; Gustilo.

## 58. Paediatric Fractures ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** Greenstick (incomplete, bent), torus/buckle (impaction), plastic deformity. Salter-Harris (physeal): I transverse through physis, II (most common, metaphysis + physis), III (physis + epiphysis, intra-articular), IV (through all 3), V (crush physis). Complications: growth arrest (III/IV/V).
- **Rapid Rev:** SALTR mnemonic: I Straight, II Above, III Lower (epi), IV Through, V cRush.
- **SBA:** Most common Salter-Harris #: **Type II**.
- **MCQ:** Type V Salter-Harris is: **compression/crush injury of physis (growth arrest)**.
- **Explanation:** Physeal plate damage → limb length discrepancy.
- **Trend:** Very high; Salter-Harris.
- **Clinical:** Child fractures.
- **Examiner Tip:** Type II = "Above" metaphysis; most common.
- **Ref:** Apley.

## 59. Fat Embolism ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** After long-bone # (femur/tibia) / pelvic. Triad: respiratory distress, petechial rash (conjunctiva/upper body), CNS confusion. Supportive; prophylaxis = early fixation.
- **Rapid Rev:** Petechiae + dyspnoea + long-bone # = fat embolism.
- **SBA:** Fat embolism typically follows: **long-bone (femur/tibia) fractures**.
- **MCQ:** Petechiae in fat embolism appear over: **conjunctivae & upper body**.
- **Explanation:** Fat globules → pulmonary + systemic emboli.
- **Trend:** Very high; triad.
- **Clinical:** Multiple #, IM nailing.
- **Examiner Tip:** Petechiae = diagnostic clue (not just hypoxia).
- **Ref:** Apley.

## 60. DVT/PE in Ortho ⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** Highest risk after hip/knee arthroplasty, pelvis, femur #. Prophylaxis LMWH ± IPC. Symptom: calf pain/swelling; PE = hypoxia/chest pain. Wells score.
- **Rapid Rev:** Chemoprophylaxis until mobilised (esp. THR/TKR).
- **SBA:** Highest VTE risk ortho procedure: **hip/knee replacement & pelvis #**.
- **MCQ:** Standard VTE prophylaxis post-THR: **LMWH + early mobilisation + stockings**.
- **Explanation:** Immobility + hypercoagulability.
- **Trend:** High; overlaps #38.
- **Clinical:** Post-op.
- **Examiner Tip:** Aspirin alone insufficient for high-risk.
- **Ref:** AAOS.

## 61. DDH ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** Developmental dysplasia of hip. Barlow (dislocatable), Ortolani (reduces click) <3 mo. Graf ultrasound <6 mo; X-ray after (acetabular index, Shenton line). Rx: Pavlik harness (infants), closed/open reduction + spica.
- **Rapid Rev:** Ortolani = CLICK reducible; Barlow = dislocatable. USG <6 mo.
- **SBA:** Positive Ortolani sign means: **dislocated hip can be reduced (click)**.
- **MCQ:** Investigation of choice for DDH <6 months: **ultrasound (Graf)**.
- **Explanation:** Cartilage not ossified → USG better than X-ray.
- **Trend:** Very high; tests + imaging.
- **Clinical:** Female, breech, firstborn.
- **Examiner Tip:** Ortolani = reducible click; Barlow = dislocatable (different!).
- **Ref:** Apley.

## 62. CTEV (Clubfoot) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** Congenital talipes equinovarus: Cavus, Adductus, Varus, Equinus. Pirani / Dimeglio score. Ponseti method = serial casting + tenotomy of Achilles; boots-and-bars maintenance.
- **Rapid Rev:** "CAVE": Cavus, Adductus, Varus, Equinus. Ponseti = gold standard.
- **SBA:** Treatment of choice for idiopathic clubfoot: **Ponseti serial casting**.
- **MCQ:** Ponseti method includes: **serial casts + percutaneous Achilles tenotomy**.
- **Explanation:** Gradual correction; tenotomy for equinus.
- **Trend:** Very high; Ponseti.
- **Clinical:** Newborn.
- **Examiner Tip:** Maintenance brace prevents relapse (most recurrences = non-compliance).
- **Ref:** Apley.

## 63. Cerebral Palsy ⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** Non-progressive motor disorder (perinatal brain injury). Spastic (most), dyskinetic, ataxic. GMFCS levels I–V. Management: botulinum toxin, serial casting, selective dorsal rhizotomy, multilevel surgery.
- **Rapid Rev:** GMFCS I (walks) → V (wheelchair). Spastic = commonest.
- **SBA:** Most common CP type: **spastic (diplegic/hemiplegic/quadriplegic)**.
- **MCQ:** Botulinum toxin in CP is used for: **spasticity (dynamic contracture)**.
- **Explanation:** Chemodenervation reduces tone.
- **Trend:** High; GMFCS.
- **Clinical:** Paediatric ortho.
- **Examiner Tip:** CP is NON-progressive (lesion static; manifestations change).
- **Ref:** Campbell.

## 64. Physeal Growth ⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** Epiphyseal plate = growth; proximal tibia/distal femur contribute most lower-limb length. Injury → partial arrest (angular) or complete (shortening). Guided growth (8-plate) for angular.
- **Rapid Rev:** Most growth: distal femur + proximal tibia.
- **SBA:** Greatest lower-limb growth from: **distal femoral & proximal tibial physes**.
- **MCQ:** Angular deformity after physeal bar treated by: **guided growth / bar resection**.
- **Explanation:** Asymmetric growth → deformity.
- **Trend:** Moderate; growth plates.
- **Clinical:** Child limb discrepancy.
- **Examiner Tip:** Distal femur physis = ~40% femoral growth.
- **Ref:** Apley.

## 65. SCFE ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** Slipped capital femoral epiphysis (adolescent obese male). Klein's line (should intersect femoral head on AP) fails → slip. Rx: pins-in-situ (percutaneous); avoid manipulative reduction (AVN risk).
- **Rapid Rev:** Klein's line through superior neck should cross head.
- **SBA:** Investigation showing SCFE: **AP pelvis with Klein's line not crossing femoral head**.
- **MCQ:** Treatment of stable SCFE: **in-situ pinning (screw)**.
- **Explanation:** Fix slip, do not reduce (AVN/CHD risk).
- **Trend:** Very high; Klein + management.
- **Clinical:** Obese adolescent, limp.
- **Examiner Tip:** Do NOT reduce the slip (causes AVN).
- **Ref:** Apley.

## 66. Perthes Disease ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** Idiopathic AVN of femoral head (4–8 yr, boys). Waldenström phases (necrosis→fragmentation→reossification→healed). Herring/Lateral-pillar classification (A/B/C). Containment (abduction brace/osteotomy) if >6 yr or C.
- **Rapid Rev:** Age >6 + lateral pillar C = poor prognosis.
- **SBA:** Lateral-pillar (Herring) C implies: **poor prognosis**.
- **MCQ:** Perthes occurs typically in: **boys 4–8 years**.
- **Explanation:** AVN remodels; older age = worse.
- **Trend:** Very high; classification.
- **Clinical:** Limp, restricted abduction/IR.
- **Examiner Tip:** Perthes = AVN in CHILD (not adult steroid AVN).
- **Ref:** Apley.

## 67. Rickets / Osteomalacia ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** Vit-D deficiency → defective mineralisation. Children: wide epiphyses, rachitic rosary, craniotabes, bowed legs, Harrison's sulcus. Adults: bone pain, Looser's zones (pseudofractures), proximal myopathy.
- **Rapid Rev:** Rickets = child (open plates); osteomalacia = adult.
- **SBA:** Rachitic rosary is seen in: **rickets (vitamin-D deficiency)**.
- **MCQ:** Looser's zones are characteristic of: **osteomalacia**.
- **Explanation:** Unmineralised osteoid → pseudofractures.
- **Trend:** Very high; BD endemic.
- **Clinical:** Nutritional, malabsorption, renal.
- **Examiner Tip:** Rickets (children) vs osteomalacia (adults) — same deficiency.
- **Ref:** Robbins.

## 68. Osteogenesis Imperfecta ⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** Type I collagen defect → brittle bones, blue sclerae, hearing loss, dentinogenesis imperfecta, wormian bones. Rx: bisphosphonates (↓ fracture), physical therapy, rodding.
- **Rapid Rev:** "Blue sclerae + multiple #" = OI.
- **SBA:** Blue sclerae are characteristic of: **osteogenesis imperfecta**.
- **MCQ:** OI is due to defect in: **type I collagen**.
- **Explanation:** Faulty collagen → fragile bone.
- **Trend:** High; type + sign.
- **Clinical:** Recurrent childhood fractures.
- **Examiner Tip:** OI = Type I (not II).
- **Ref:** Robbins.

## 69. Achondroplasia & Dysplasias ⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** Most common dwarfism; FGFR3 mutation → inhibited endochondral ossification → short limbs, normal trunk, macrocephaly, rhizomelic shortening. Foramen magnum/spinal stenosis risk.
- **Rapid Rev:** "Short limbs, normal trunk" = achondroplasia.
- **SBA:** Achondroplasia causes: **defective endochondral ossification (FGFR3)**.
- **MCQ:** In achondroplasia, trunk is: **normal / relatively long (limbs short)**.
- **Explanation:** Membranous (skull/trunk) spared; endochondral (long bones) impaired.
- **Trend:** High; features.
- **Clinical:** Dwarfism.
- **Examiner Tip:** Rhizomelic = proximal segments shorter.
- **Ref:** Robbins.

## 70. Congenital Pseudarthrosis Tibia ⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** Rare; anterolateral bow + pseudoarthrosis (often NF1). Rx: Ilizarov, intramedullary rod, bone graft; high non-union.
- **Rapid Rev:** Tibial anterolateral bow + pseudoarthrosis.
- **SBA:** Congenital pseudoarthrosis is most common in: **tibia**.
- **MCQ:** Associated condition: **neurofibromatosis type 1**.
- **Explanation:** Dysplastic anterior tibia → fracture → non-union.
- **Trend:** Moderate; rare.
- **Clinical:** Child.
- **Examiner Tip:** Think NF1 when you see it.
- **Ref:** Apley.

## 71. Cervical Spine ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** Cord syndromes: central (central canal → upper > lower limb, hyperextension in spondylosis), Brown-Séquard (hemisection → ipsilateral motor+position, contralateral pain/temp). Atlanto-axial (odontoid) # in elderly falls.
- **Rapid Rev:** Central = arms worse; Brown-Séquard = same-side motor.
- **SBA:** Ipsilateral weakness + contralateral pain loss = **Brown-Séquard syndrome**.
- **MCQ:** Hyperextension in cervical spondylosis → **central cord syndrome**.
- **Explanation:** Central grey matter affected → upper limb > lower.
- **Trend:** Very high; syndromes.
- **Clinical:** Elderly fall, trauma.
- **Examiner Tip:** Central cord = upper limbs affected more.
- **Ref:** Apley.

## 72. Lumbar Disc Prolapse ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** L4–L5 (L5 root) & L5–S1 (S1 root) most common. Sciatica; straight-leg raise (SLR) +ve 30–60°; crossed SLR specific. Rx: conservative; surgery if cauda equina/deficit.
- **Rapid Rev:** SLR +ve = disc. L5 = foot drop/big toe; S1 = plantarflexion/ankle jerk.
- **SBA:** Positive straight-leg raise indicates: **lumbar disc prolapse**.
- **MCQ:** Most common level of disc prolapse: **L4–L5**.
- **Explanation:** Posterolateral herniation compresses traversing root.
- **Trend:** Very high; SLR.
- **Clinical:** Sciatica.
- **Examiner Tip:** Crossed SLR (opposite leg) = more specific.
- **Ref:** Apley.

## 73. Spondylolisthesis ⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** Anterior slip of vertebra (isthmic = pars defect L5/S1; degenerative). Meyerding I–IV (≤25% to >75%). Slipped >50% (III/IV) → surgery.
- **Rapid Rev:** Isthmic = pars interarticularis defect.
- **SBA:** Isthmic spondylolisthesis is due to: **pars interarticularis defect**.
- **MCQ:** Meyerding grade III = slip of: **50–75%**.
- **Explanation:** Slip graded by % of vertebral width.
- **Trend:** High; classification.
- **Clinical:** Spondylolysis (pars #) in athletes.
- **Examiner Tip:** Pars defect = isthmic (not degenerative).
- **Ref:** Apley.

## 74. Scoliosis ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** Lateral curvature + rotation. Idiopathic (adolescent, girls), congenital, neuromuscular. Cobb angle (>10° diagnostic). Bracing (Milwaukee/TLSO) for 25–45° growing; surgery >45–50°.
- **Rapid Rev:** Cobb angle = standard measure. Brace 25–45°, surgery >45°.
- **SBA:** Scoliosis is measured by: **Cobb angle**.
- **MCQ:** Indication for bracing in adolescent idiopathic scoliosis: **25–45° with growth remaining**.
- **Explanation:** Halts progression until skeletal maturity.
- **Trend:** Very high; Cobb + brace.
- **Clinical:** Screening adolescent girls.
- **Examiner Tip:** Cobb >10° = scoliosis; >45° = surgery.
- **Ref:** Apley.

## 75. Kyphosis & Scheuermann's ⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** Normal thoracic kyphosis 20–40°; >50° pathological. Scheuermann's = anterior wedging ≥5° of ≥3 vertebrae (juvenile); postural vs structural.
- **Rapid Rev:** Scheuermann = wedging of 3+ vertebrae.
- **SBA:** Scheuermann's kyphosis shows: **anterior wedging of ≥3 vertebrae ≥5°**.
- **MCQ:** Structural kyphosis >50° suggests: **pathological (Scheuermann's / TB)**.
- **Explanation:** Vertebral wedging → rigid kyphosis.
- **Trend:** Moderate; definition.
- **Clinical:** Adolescent.
- **Examiner Tip:** Postural kyphosis corrects on extension; Scheuermann's does not.
- **Ref:** Apley.

## 76. Spinal TB (Pott's) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** Mycobacterium tuberculosis; paradiscal spread from vertebral body → disc destruction + vertebral collapse → angular (gibbus) kyphosis; cold abscess (psoas/paravertebral); cord/paraplegia. RX: ATT 12–18 mo; surgery if neurologic/instability.
- **Rapid Rev:** "Cold abscess + angular kyphosis" = Pott's. ATT first.
- **SBA:** Angular kyphosis with cold abscess suggests: **spinal tuberculosis (Pott's)**.
- **MCQ:** Spread in spinal TB is: **paradiscal (from body to disc)**.
- **Explanation:** Disc destroyed → collapse → gibbus.
- **Trend:** Very high; BD endemic.
- **Clinical:** Dorsal spine, paraplegia.
- **Examiner Tip:** Pott's = COLD abscess (no warmth/redness).
- **Ref:** Apley; Robbins.

## 77. Spinal Stenosis ⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** Narrowing of spinal canal → neurogenic claudication (pain on standing/walking, relieved by sitting/forward flexion). Lumbar common. Rx: decompressive laminectomy.
- **Rapid Rev:** Neurogenic claudication = spinal stenosis; vascular = calf, relieved by rest not posture.
- **SBA:** Pain on standing relieved by flexion = **spinal stenosis (neurogenic claudication)**.
- **MCQ:** Spinal stenosis is best confirmed by: **MRI/CT**.
- **Explanation:** Position-dependent symptoms = hallmark.
- **Trend:** High; claudication differentiation.
- **Clinical:** Elderly.
- **Examiner Tip:** Vascular claudication = calf, rest relieves; neurogenic = posture relieves.
- **Ref:** Apley.

## 78. Cauda Equina Syndrome ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** Emergency: saddle anaesthesia, bladder/bowel incontinence, bilateral radiculopathy, weak S1. Causes: massive disc, tumour, trauma, abscess. Rx: urgent decompression (<48 h).
- **Rapid Rev:** Saddle anaesthesia + urinary retention = RED FLAG.
- **SBA:** Cauda equina syndrome includes: **saddle anaesthesia + urinary retention**.
- **MCQ:** Management of cauda equina: **urgent surgical decompression**.
- **Explanation:** Permanent incontinence if delayed.
- **Trend:** Very high; emergency.
- **Clinical:** Large central L-disc, tumour.
- **Examiner Tip:** Urinary retention = surgical emergency (not conservative).
- **Ref:** Apley.

## 79. Osteoarthritis ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** Degenerative; cartilage loss, osteophytes, subchondral sclerosis/cysts, joint-space narrowing (JSN). Knee > hip > hand. Kellgren-Lawrence grading. Rx: weight loss, physio, analgesia, joint replacement.
- **Rapid Rev:** JSN + osteophytes = OA. Heberden (DIP)/Bouchard (PIP) nodes.
- **SBA:** Radiologic feature of OA: **joint-space narrowing + osteophytes**.
- **MCQ:** Kellgren-Lawrence classifies: **severity of osteoarthritis**.
- **Explanation:** Cartilage wear → bone-on-bone.
- **Trend:** Very high; X-ray signs.
- **Clinical:** Elderly, obesity.
- **Examiner Tip:** OA = JSN (not osteoporosis). MCP spared (unlike RA).
- **Ref:** Apley.

## 80. Rheumatoid Arthritis ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** Autoimmune, symmetrical polyarthritis; MCP/PIP, wrist; swan-neck (PIP ext, DIP flex), boutonnière (PIP flex, DIP ext); erosions, ulnar deviation. Cervical subluxation (atlanto-axial) = airway risk. DMARDs (MTX), biologics.
- **Rapid Rev:** RA = MCP/PIP (NOT DIP); Swan-neck vs Boutonnière.
- **SBA:** Cervical subluxation is a complication of: **rheumatoid arthritis**.
- **MCQ:** Swan-neck deformity = **PIP hyperextension + DIP flexion**.
- **Explanation:** Synovitis erodes joints → deformities.
- **Trend:** Very high; deformities + C-spine.
- **Clinical:** Hands, feet.
- **Examiner Tip:** RA spares DIP (PsA affects DIP).
- **Ref:** Apley.

## 81. Septic Arthritis ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** Hot, swollen, restricted joint; neonate/elderly, immuno-compromised. S. aureus #1; gonococcus in young sexually active; H. influenzae in unvaccinated children. Dx: aspirate (Gram, culture) BEFORE ABx; urgent drainage + ABx.
- **Rapid Rev:** "Hot joint → aspirate first, then ABx." Knee common.
- **SBA:** Investigation of choice for septic arthritis: **joint aspiration + culture**.
- **MCQ:** Most common organism in septic arthritis: **S. aureus**.
- **Explanation:** Bacteria seed synovium → purulent arthritis.
- **Trend:** Very high; organism + aspiration.
- **Clinical:** Knee, hip (hip = urgent).
- **Examiner Tip:** Aspirate BEFORE starting antibiotics.
- **Ref:** Apley; Robbins.

## 82. Gout & Pseudogout ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** Gout = monosodium urate, negatively birefringent needles, 1st MTP (podagra), tophi. Pseudogout (CPPD) = calcium pyrophosphate, positively birefringent rhomboids, knee, chondrocalcinosis. Acute: NSAIDs/colchicine/steroids; prophylaxis: allopurinol/febuxostat.
- **Rapid Rev:** Gout = NEGATIVE needles; Pseudogout = POSITIVE rhomboids.
- **SBA:** Negatively birefringent crystals = **gout**.
- **MCQ:** Pseudogout crystals are: **calcium pyrophosphate (positive birefringence)**.
- **Explanation:** Crystal type differentiates.
- **Trend:** Very high; crystal sign.
- **Clinical:** Podagra, renal stones.
- **Examiner Tip:** Pseudogout = knees + chondrocalcinosis (not 1st MTP).
- **Ref:** Robbins.

## 83. Ankylosing Spondylitis ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** Seronegative spondyloarthropathy; HLA-B27; sacroiliitis → bamboo spine; enthesitis; uveitis; reduced chest expansion. Schober test <4 cm (normal ≥5). Rx: NSAIDs, physiotherapy, TNF inhibitors.
- **Rapid Rev:** Bamboo spine + HLA-B27. Schober <4 cm.
- **SBA:** Bamboo spine is characteristic of: **ankylosing spondylitis**.
- **MCQ:** Schober test assesses: **lumbar flexion (AS limited)**.
- **Explanation:** Spinal fusion → stiffness.
- **Trend:** Very high; HLA-B27 + Schober.
- **Clinical:** Young male, back stiffness.
- **Examiner Tip:** Schober <4 cm = limited flexion (not >4).
- **Ref:** Apley.

## 84. Avascular Necrosis ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** Ischaemic necrosis; femoral head (steroids, alcohol, trauma, SLE), scaphoid proximal pole, talus, medial femoral condyle. MRI earliest. Rx: core decompression early, arthroplasty late.
- **Rapid Rev:** "ASEPTIC": Alcohol, Steroids, Trauma, Idiopathic, Caisson, Lupus, Sickle.
- **SBA:** Gold-standard investigation for early AVN: **MRI**.
- **MCQ:** Most common cause of femoral-head AVN: **steroids / fracture neck femur**.
- **Explanation:** X-ray normal early; MRI shows oedema.
- **Trend:** Very high; MRI + causes.
- **Clinical:** Steroid use, trauma.
- **Examiner Tip:** MRI, not X-ray, for early AVN.
- **Ref:** Apley.

## 85. Charcot Joint ⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** Neuropathic joint destruction from lost sensation (diabetes, syphilis, spinal cord injury). Pain-disproportionate to X-ray destruction; "bag of bones". Rx: offloading, braces; surgery late.
- **Rapid Rev:** "Looks terrible, hurts little" = Charcot.
- **SBA:** Charcot joint is due to: **neuropathy (loss of sensation)**.
- **MCQ:** Most common cause of Charcot joint today: **diabetes mellitus**.
- **Explanation:** Repeated unnoticed trauma → destruction.
- **Trend:** High; diabetes link.
- **Clinical:** Diabetic foot.
- **Examiner Tip:** Charcot = neuropathic (not septic) though can superinfect.
- **Ref:** Apley.

## 86. Hemophilic Arthropathy ⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** Recurrent haemarthrosis → synovitis, cartilage loss, arthropathy (knee/elbow/ankle). Prophylactic factor VIII/IX. Rx: factor replacement, aspiration (caution), synovectomy.
- **Rapid Rev:** Haemarthrosis → "target joint."
- **SBA:** Hemophilic arthropathy results from: **recurrent intra-articular haemorrhage**.
- **MCQ:** Target joints in haemophilia: **knee, elbow, ankle**.
- **Explanation:** Iron deposition → synovitis → damage.
- **Trend:** High; mechanism.
- **Clinical:** Haemophilia A/B.
- **Examiner Tip:** Prophylactic factor prevents arthropathy.
- **Ref:** Apley.

## 87. ACL / PCL ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** ACL: ant drawer, Lachman (most sensitive), pivot shift; non-contact valgus+rotation. PCL: post drawer; dashboard. Rx: reconstruction (hamstring/patellar graft).
- **Rapid Rev:** Lachman = best test. Pivot shift = rotatory.
- **SBA:** Most sensitive test for ACL tear: **Lachman test**.
- **MCQ:** Mechanism of ACL injury: **non-contact pivoting / valgus rotation**.
- **Explanation:** ACL resists anterior tibial translation.
- **Trend:** Very high; Lachman.
- **Clinical:** Sports.
- **Examiner Tip:** Lachman > anterior drawer (more sensitive).
- **Ref:** Apley.

## 88. Meniscal Injury ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** Medial (>lateral) due to MCL attachment. McMurray (click), Apley (compression/rotation), Thessaly. Bucket-handle = locked knee. Rx: arthroscopic repair (peripheral) / partial meniscectomy.
- **Rapid Rev:** Medial meniscus torn more; bucket-handle = lock.
- **SBA:** Bucket-handle meniscal tear causes: **locked knee**.
- **MCQ:** McMurray test assesses: **meniscal tear**.
- **Explanation:** Torn fragment clicks on rotation.
- **Trend:** Very high; tests.
- **Clinical:** Twisting injury.
- **Examiner Tip:** Medial meniscus = fixed = more injured.
- **Ref:** Apley.

## 89. Rotator Cuff ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** Supraspinatus (abduction 0–15°, impingement), infraspinatus/teres minor (ext rot), subscapularis (int rot). Supraspinatus most torn. Drop-arm test; painful arc 60–120°. Rx: physio, subacromial decompression, repair.
- **Rapid Rev:** "SITS". Supraspinatus = most commonly torn/impinged.
- **SBA:** Most commonly torn rotator cuff tendon: **supraspinatus**.
- **MCQ:** Painful arc (60–120°) suggests: **subacromial impingement / supraspinatus tendinopathy**.
- **Explanation:** Tendon compressed under acromion in mid-range.
- **Trend:** Very high; cuff anatomy.
- **Clinical:** Overhead activity, elderly.
- **Examiner Tip:** Drop-arm = supraspinatus/large cuff tear.
- **Ref:** Apley.

## 90. Tendinopathy ⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** Tennis elbow = lateral epicondylitis (extensor carpi radialis brevis); golfer's = medial. Achilles tendinopathy/rupture (Thompson test -ve squeeze). Rx: eccentric exercise, rest, NSAID, surgery.
- **Rapid Rev:** Tennis = lateral; golfer = medial. Thompson -ve = Achilles rupture.
- **SBA:** Tennis elbow involves: **lateral epicondyle (ECRB)**.
- **MCQ:** Positive Thompson test (no plantarflex on calf squeeze) = **Achilles tendon rupture**.
- **Explanation:** Loss of continuity → no reflex plantarflex.
- **Trend:** High; epicondyle + Thompson.
- **Clinical:** Overuse.
- **Examiner Tip:** Thompson -ve (absent movement) = rupture.
- **Ref:** Apley.

## 91. Osteosarcoma ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** Most common primary malignant bone tumour in children/young. Metaphysis (distal femur, proximal tibia, proximal humerus). Sunburst periosteal reaction, Codman triangle. Lung metastases. Rx: neoadjuvant chemo + wide resection.
- **Rapid Rev:** Metaphysis + sunburst + Codman = osteosarcoma.
- **SBA:** Sunburst appearance on X-ray = **osteosarcoma**.
- **MCQ:** Most common primary malignant bone tumour in children: **osteosarcoma**.
- **Explanation:** Aggressive osteoid-producing tumour.
- **Trend:** Very high; imaging sign.
- **Clinical:** Adolescent knee pain/swelling.
- **Examiner Tip:** Osteosarcoma = METAPHYSIS (not diaphysis).
- **Ref:** Robbins; Campbell.

## 92. Ewing's Sarcoma ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** Diaphysis (long bones, pelvis); onion-skin periosteal reaction; t(11;22) EWS-FLI1; <20 yr; fever/ESR↑ (mimics infection). Rx: chemo + radiation/surgery.
- **Rapid Rev:** Diaphysis + onion-skin + t(11;22) = Ewing's.
- **SBA:** Onion-skin periosteal reaction = **Ewing's sarcoma**.
- **MCQ:** Chromosomal translocation in Ewing's: **t(11;22)**.
- **Explanation:** Small round blue cells.
- **Trend:** Very high; sign + genetics.
- **Clinical:** Child/teen, fever.
- **Examiner Tip:** Ewing = DIAPHYSIS (vs osteosarcoma metaphysis).
- **Ref:** Robbins.

## 93. Osteomyelitis (Paediatric) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** Acute haematogenous; metaphysis (femur/distal femur); S. aureus. Neonates: GBS/E. coli. Sickle: Salmonella. Subacute: Brodie's abscess. Chronic: sequestrum + involucrum.
- **Rapid Rev:** Kids = metaphysis; sequestrum = dead bone.
- **SBA:** Most common site of paediatric haematogenous osteomyelitis: **metaphysis (distal femur)**.
- **MCQ:** Sequestrum is: **necrotic dead bone within infected focus**.
- **Explanation:** Ischaemic bone dies → sequestrum.
- **Trend:** Very high; site + terms.
- **Clinical:** Fever, localised pain, limp.
- **Examiner Tip:** Involucrum = new living shell (not dead).
- **Ref:** Robbins.

## 94. Bone Metastasis ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** Most common malignant bone tumour overall. Prostate = osteoblastic (sclerotic). Breast/lung/thyroid/kidney = lytic. Rx: bisphosphonate, radio, stabilisation.
- **Rapid Rev:** Prostate = blastic; breast/lung = lytic.
- **SBA:** Sclerotic (blastic) metastasis suggests: **prostate carcinoma**.
- **MCQ:** Most common bone metastases are from: **breast, prostate, lung, kidney, thyroid**.
- **Explanation:** Blastic vs lytic pattern hints primary.
- **Trend:** Very high; prostate/breast.
- **Clinical:** Back pain, pathological #.
- **Examiner Tip:** Prostate = blastic (not lytic).
- **Ref:** Robbins.

## 95. Giant-Cell Tumour ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** Epiphysis (around knee — distal femur/proximal tibia), 20–40 yr. Soap-bubble / expansile lytic; Campanacci grading. "Osteoclastoma." Rx: curettage + cement/graft; denosumab; malignant transformation rare.
- **Rapid Rev:** Epiphysis + soap-bubble = GCT.
- **SBA:** Soap-bubble appearance = **giant-cell tumour**.
- **MCQ:** Giant-cell tumour typically occurs in: **epiphysis (around knee)**.
- **Explanation:** Locally aggressive, rarely malignant.
- **Trend:** Very high; location + sign.
- **Clinical:** Young adult knee.
- **Examiner Tip:** GCT = EPIPHYSIS (not metaphysis like osteosarcoma).
- **Ref:** Robbins.

## 96. Soft-Tissue Sarcoma ⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** Deep, >5 cm, painful, fixed; staging size/depth/grade + metastasis (lung). Rx: incisional biopsy then wide (compartment) excision ± radiotherapy; limb-salvage.
- **Rapid Rev:** "Deep + >5 cm" → sarcoma workup.
- **SBA:** Management principle for suspected soft-tissue sarcoma: **planned incisional biopsy then wide excision**.
- **MCQ:** Most common site of metastasis: **lung**.
- **Explanation:** Bad biopsy worsens outcome.
- **Trend:** High; biopsy principle.
- **Clinical:** Extremity mass.
- **Examiner Tip:** Plan biopsy with oncologic surgeon.
- **Ref:** Schwartz; Campbell.

## 97. Polytrauma (Ortho) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** Multiple injuries; ATLS; damage-control ortho (external fixator temporising for femur/pelvis); prioritise life > limb; early total care vs damage control.
- **Rapid Rev:** Life before limb. Temporary fixator then definitive.
- **SBA:** In polytrauma with femur #, initial stabilisation: **external fixator (damage control)**.
- **MCQ:** Damage-control ortho aims to: **temporise fixation to allow resuscitation**.
- **Explanation:** Avoids prolonged anaesthesia in unstable patient.
- **Trend:** High; trauma.
- **Clinical:** RTA.
- **Examiner Tip:** Convert to IM nail once stable.
- **Ref:** ATLS; Rockwood.

## 98. Osteoporosis ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** Low bone mass; DEXA T-score ≤−2.5. Post-menopausal (type I) & senile (type II). Rx: bisphosphonate, Ca²⁺/vit D, fall prevention. Atypical femur # (bisphosphonate). Vertebral/wrist/hip #.
- **Rapid Rev:** T-score ≤−2.5 = osteoporosis. T −1 to −2.5 = osteopenia.
- **SBA:** Diagnosis of osteoporosis: **DEXA T-score ≤ −2.5**.
- **MCQ:** Bisphosphonates are first-line for: **osteoporosis**.
- **Explanation:** ↓ resorption → ↑ density.
- **Trend:** Very high; DEXA.
- **Clinical:** Elderly women.
- **Examiner Tip:** Osteopenia = T −1 to −2.5 (not osteoporosis).
- **Ref:** Apley.

## 99. Imaging in Ortho ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** X-ray: 2 views (AP/lat) always; stress/oblique as needed. CT: complex #, pelvis, spine. MRI: soft tissue, AVN, disc, tumour, infection. Bone scan: metastases, stress #, occult. USG: DDH <6 mo, soft tissue, effusion.
- **Rapid Rev:** X-ray 1st; MRI soft tissue/AVN; bone scan metastases.
- **SBA:** Investigation of choice for AVN: **MRI**.
- **MCQ:** DDH <6 months: **ultrasound**.
- **Explanation:** Modality matched to tissue.
- **Trend:** Very high; "investigation of choice" stems.
- **Clinical:** All ortho.
- **Examiner Tip:** Bone scan sensitive but not specific.
- **Ref:** Apley.

## 100. Principles of Fracture Fixation ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Notes:** AO/ASIF principles: anatomical reduction, stable fixation, preservation of blood supply (biological), early motion. Absolute stability (compression plate) vs relative stability (IM nail/ex-fix). ORIF vs MIPO/BO (biological osteosynthesis). Tension band (patella, olecranon).
- **Rapid Rev:** AO = reduce, fix, preserve biology, move early.
- **SBA:** AO principle emphasising biology: **preserve blood supply (minimally invasive / biological fixation)**.
- **MCQ:** Tension-band principle used for: **patella & olecranon fractures**.
- **Explanation:** Converts tension → compression at fracture.
- **Trend:** Very high; principles.
- **Clinical:** Every #.
- **Examiner Tip:** Absolute stability = compression; relative = IM nail.
- **Ref:** AO Principles.
